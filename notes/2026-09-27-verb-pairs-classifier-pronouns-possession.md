## 1. Action verbs: verb + its everyday noun
An action verb can stand alone but is almost always said with a given noun. The pair
names the activity (taug = walk along + kev = road → *taug kev*, walking).
- **Lesson** (action-verbs.js): "Many verbs bring their own object" → **"Verb + its
  everyday noun"**, with the seven pairs and a literal breakdown: taug kev, noj mov,
  nyeem ntawv, sau ntawv, kawm ntawv, mloog lus, hais lus. A new examples step
  "Verb + noun pairs".
- **Cards** (Verbs set): noj mov, nyeem ntawv, sau ntawv, mloog lus, hais lus (kawm
  ntawv and taug kev existed). They're tagged `construction`, so the sentence builder
  keeps verb and noun as two chips. *mloog lus* "do as you are told" is TODO-VERIFY.

## 2. Classifiers as pronouns + the author's no / ko / ntawd
- **no**: right here, the one in focus · **ko**: near or next to something or
  someone · **ntawd**: there, over there, at a distance. These replace the old
  TODO-VERIFY ko/ntawd split, in the lesson and on the three cards.
- A classifier works like an article; before no/ko/ntawd it replaces the noun and
  becomes a pronoun: *phau no* (this one), *cov ko* (those ones), *lub ntawd* (that
  one). The demonstratives lesson has a new section with the author's six example
  pairs, plus a pattern card "classifier + no / ko / ntawd". The pronouns lesson
  gains a short "Classifiers can be pronouns too" section pointing on.
