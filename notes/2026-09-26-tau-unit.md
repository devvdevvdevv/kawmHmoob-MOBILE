## Three decisions worth knowing

1. **The cards are constructions, not words.** Each card is a pattern (*noj tau*),
   so the flashcards drill word order: *tau noj* (ate) next to *noj tau* (was able
   to eat).
2. **`construction` tag → not a chip.** The sentence builder turns every multi-word
   dictionary entry into ONE chip. That made *Kuv tau noj mov* into
   [Kuv] [tau noj] [mov] in every sentence in the app, so the learner never placed
   *tau*. Cards tagged `construction` are now skipped by `buildPhraseIndex`, and
   *tau* is always its own chip. Lookup still sees them (a tap on *tau* in
   "tau noj" shows the pattern + word by word).
3. **No word in two units.** The base `tau` card (Time Markers) and `tsis tau`
   (Not & Don't) were in the first draft of the unit. check-path rejects that
   (double-counted progress, "I already learned this"). They stay in their own
   units; the lesson still teaches both.

## Fact-check corrections (author ran the logic past GPT, same day)
1. **Preverbal tau = attainment/fulfilment marker**, not simply past tense. It's
   translated as English past ("did, have done") when the context is past.
2. **V + tau is primarily modal: "can, be able to"**. It's "was able to" in a past
   context, and there's a separate **accomplishment** reading ("managed to,
   succeeded": *Nws kawm tau*).
3. **"Kuv mus tau does NOT mean I went" was too absolute.** It isn't the normal
   way to say "I went" (*Kuv tau mus*); its usual meaning is "I can go", and context
   still decides.

Applied everywhere:
- Pattern cards: *tau + verb* "the action is attained — it happened; in a past
  context, did, have done"; *verb + tau* "can, be able to — in a past context, was
  able to; with some verbs, managed to", example *Kuv noj tau* = **I can eat**. "In
  real words" adds *kawm tau* (accomplishment).
- Examples: *Kuv noj tau* / *Kuv mus tau* now "I can eat / I can go"; noj/mus/ua/pom
  tau glosses → "can, be able to"; *kawm tau* labelled as the accomplishment
  reading.
- The tau dictionary entry: sense 1 "did, have done: the action is attained";
  sense 5 "can, be able to … in a past context, I was able to go"; **new sense 6**
  "managed to, succeeded in (after some verbs)" (appended, so pins @1 / @3 are
  unchanged).
- The lesson: the "Before the verb" section now says attained/not simply past; the
  "After the verb" section says can/be able to, context readings and the
  accomplishment example; the absolute sentence is softened; the remember list and
  example items are updated.
- Tense Markers lesson + Speak card: "Did, have done (attained, completed)". The
  verb + tau note now reads "usually means can: Kuv noj tau — I can eat".

## ✅ Settled: "can't" is VERB + TSIS TAU (the author's GPT review)
The author doubted the tsis + verb + tau change ("tau is so confusing") and ran it
past GPT. The review (with sources: Nws mus tsis tau = "He can't go"; Study Hmong's
*Kuv hais lus Askiv tsis tau* = "I can't speak English"):
- **V + tsis tau** is the standard negative potential, "can't / not able to /
  wasn't able to / not permitted", by context.
- **tsis tau + V** is negative attainment, "hasn't / didn't".
- **tsis + V + tau** is *not* the normal "can't". It leans to a negative
  accomplishment ("did not manage to"). It isn't taught as the target form.
- The object goes after: **V + tsis tau + object**, *Kuv nrhiav tsis tau lwm txoj
  kev*. (*…lwm txoj kev tsis tau* also occurs, but keeping tau near the verb is
  more idiomatic.)
- It gave no quantitative/regional evidence, so neither form is labelled regional.

Reverted accordingly: the pattern card is back to **verb + tsis tau** (id
`tau-pattern-verb-tsis-tau`), and the examples are back to *Kuv noj / mus / nqa tsis
tau*. There's +1 example, *Kuv nrhiav tsis tau lwm txoj kev* (TODO count 845). The
Not & Don't card and the tsis lesson note are back to "after a verb: can't". The tau
lesson now teaches verb + tsis tau (+ object) and contrasts *nrhiav tsis tau lwm txoj
kev* with *tsis tau nrhiav lwm txoj kev*. It also notes that tsis + verb + tau is heard
but leans to "did not manage to". **The author's four story lines (nrhiav / piav /
caum tsis tau) were correct all along**, so they're unchanged. The GPT reply seemed cut
off after question 1: the standalone *Tsis tau* / *Tau lawm* questions and the sentence
checks are still unanswered.

- **Author ruling after the review:** "then tsis + verb + tau is incorrect." The lesson line now says don't use it for can't, and shows the three side by side (Kuv tsis tau mus ✓ haven't gone / Kuv mus tsis tau ✓ can't go / Kuv tsis mus tau ✗). Memory `hmong-tsis-before-verb` rewritten as settled.
