# New story: "Mus Saib Yeeb Yam" (Going to the Movies), a pronoun story (2026-09-26)

**Written by the author**, with grammar polished with AI by the author. It is
live in the **Everyday Life** shelf at Beginner level (about 12 minutes). It
shows the difference between **all nine pronouns** in one scene where the
groupings keep changing: the narrator + Fong = *wb*; Mai + Nraug = *nkawd*;
all four = *peb*; addressing two = *neb*, all = *nej*.

## What was imported (`src/data/stories.js`, id `story-mus-saib-yeeb-yam`)
- **The Hmong and English are verbatim:** 21 paragraphs, **164 sentence pairs**,
  lined up automatically. One exception: in ¶7, "Thank you, you two. Now we know
  the seat numbers." was joined into one English line, to pair with its single
  Hmong sentence. The original text is in `_incoming/story-mus-saib-yeeb-yam/`
  (`hmong.txt`, `english.txt`, `aligned.json`).
- **⚠️ The pronouns are the author's deliberate choices.** A first review
  flagged narration like *"Yaj hais rau nej"* against the English "to us". The
  author: "there are no pronoun mistakes, I purposefully put the pronouns like
  there so it's specific and used correctly context wise." **Nothing was
  changed.** This is saved to memory as a standing rule.
- **Glossary:** 51 entries, from the author's table, corrected **only where it
  disagreed with the text**. The table said *sib cais*, but the text has *sib
  nrug*; *tib pliag* became *ib pliag*; *vim li ntawd* (not in the text) became
  *yog li*. Six names were added (Fong, Mai, Nraug, Yaj, Ntxawm, Nplooj). They are
  glossary-only, per the house rule.
- **Quiz:** 8 questions drafted by Claude. They are in English, quote only the
  author's Hmong, and mostly ask **who a pronoun refers to** (*wb*, *nkawd*,
  *neb*, *peb*), plus Yaj's role, Mai's seat, the tickets, and who they meet.

## Dictionary
A tap test over all **2,213 words**: only **txhij** (in *npaj txhij*) and
**yeej** (in *yeej nyiam*) resolved to nothing. Both were added
(`rw-npaj-txhij`, `rw-yeej`) with the author's lines as examples. Every other
word resolves. The TODO unreviewed count was then 797.

**Second pass (same day, "add any words not in the dictionary").** A strict scan
for words with no entry of their own (a tap could still land on a compound
like *nas tsaug* or *meej pem*, which is misleading). Eight were added, each with
the author's own line as its example:
*nyiam* (to like, `verbs`), *tus lej* (number, `numbers`), *chav* (room,
`household-rooms`), *pem* (up ahead, as in *pem hauv ntej*, `locations-prepositions`),
and *zaus*, *nrug*, *tam sim no* and *ib nrab* (in the reading-* groups).
Skipped on purpose: bound halves that only appear inside a phrase that already
resolves (*ke*, *tsaug*, *ntej*, *sis*, *pliag*, *zem*, *txhij*), and the names.
All eight are tagged `unreviewed`; the TODO count is now **805**.
