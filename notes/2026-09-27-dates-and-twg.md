## Writing Dates (`u-dates`, order 26, after Days & Months; Pro)
The author: dates get their own path. Western order (month first) is the focus; Asian
order (day first) is also acceptable. The labels, the author's "classifiers for dates":
**hnub tim** (date), **hnub** (day), **xyoo** (year), each IN FRONT of its number.
- **Set `dates`** (7): hnub tim + number, xyoo + number, month + hnub tim + xyoo
  (Western), hnub tim + month + xyoo (Asian), hnub tim pes tsawg? (what date?),
  **hnub yug** (birthday, new to the dictionary), hnub no yog … (today is …). 7 builder
  sentences; the month and *hnub tim* stay single chips, and numbers are separate.
- **Lesson** `dates.js` (`grammar-dates`): the three labels, Western order (*Lub ib hlis
  ntuj hnub tim ob, xyoo ob txhiab neesnkaum rau* = January 2, 2026), Asian order, asking
  the date, birthdays, and a remember list. There are **4 practice questions** ("test their
  knowledge"): write Jan 2 2026, read *lub xya hlis ntuj hnub tim plaub*, which label
  goes before the year, and how to ask the date. The builder is the "create" half.
- ✅ **Settled:** the author confirmed the "rau" in "Lub rau ib hlis ntuj" was a typo. January is *Lub ib hlis ntuj*, as written.

## twg: classifier + twg (the author)
"twg would be appended to the relevant classifier depending on the context and the noun"
— *lub twg* is NOT the one right answer. The author's examples: *Koj tus phooj ywg yog tus
twg?* · *Nkawd yog leej twg?* · *Cov khoom nyob qhov twg?*
- `questions-which.js`: a new "## Important: twg joins the classifier of the noun you are
  asking about" leads the lesson; the section titles now say "Things that take lub" /
  "People and animals that take tus".
