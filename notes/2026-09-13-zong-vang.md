# Kev Tua Zong Vang Uas Txaus Ntshai — the first non-fiction in the library (2026-09-13)

**54 paragraphs, 166 lines, 193 glossary entries, 26 questions.** The longest
text in the app by a factor of four, and the first about real, named, living
people.

⚠️ **Retitled the same day**, from *Nco Txog Zong Vang* ("Remembering Zong
Vang") to **Kev Tua Zong Vang Uas Txaus Ntshai** — *The Horrifying Murder of
Zong Vang*, at the author's request. The **id did not change**: it is still
`story-zong-vang`, because the bookmark and the completed-step record save
against it. An id is an address, not a label — the same rule as
`misc-tus-xov` in `vocabulary.js` (see `notes/2026-09-13-xov-takes-lub.md`).

⚠️ **The Hmong title is MINE, not the author's.** `kev tua` (the killing of) +
`uas txaus ntshai` (that is horrifying — the phrase the Horror shelf already
uses). The relative `uas` and the word order are the parts a fluent reader
should check; a shorter *Kev Tua Zong Vang Txaus Ntshai* also reads.

It also needed a new shelf. It is not everyday life, not horror, and not a folk
tale — so `GENRES` gained **True accounts** (`history`, `bg-stone-700`).
Membership is derived from the genre id, so a shelf costs one line; filing a
real child's death under "Everyday life" would have cost more.

The author's terminology table is the most valuable thing in the file. It was
supplied *so the legal vocabulary stays consistent across the piece* — `kev tua
neeg txhob txwm thawj theem` is first-degree intentional homicide everywhere it
appears, not three near-synonyms. Rewording any of those to read better would
destroy exactly what it is for.

The English is a **back-translation**: the Hmong is itself a translation of an
English-language account, so I am recovering an original rather than inventing
one. That is a safer job than translating an original text — but no fluent reader
has checked a line of it.

## An age always takes `muaj`

The author's correction, and it is a general rule, not a fix to one line:

> **An age in Hmong is `muaj <number> xyoos` — "has N years".** The verb is not
> optional, even in a list where English drops it.

The five list items read `Omer Ninham, kaum plaub xyoos`. They now read
`Omer Ninham, muaj kaum plaub xyoos`.

⚠️ **The author's own prose already followed the rule everywhere else** — *"ib
tug tub hluas Hmoob muaj kaum peb xyoos"*, *"thaum nws muaj kaum plaub xyoos"*.
Only the bulleted list broke it, and that is the tell: **a list item looks like a
label, so the verb feels droppable.** It is still a predicate. This is the
mistake a fluent English speaker makes when the surrounding sentences would have
told them the answer.

`check-notes.mjs` asserts no line in any story has a bare `<number> xyoos`
without `muaj`, because the next list of people is where it comes back.

## Three editorial decisions, all of which could have gone wrong quietly

**1. The three uncharged minors stay abbreviated.** The author wrote `Jeffrey
P.`, `Amanda G.`, `Christin J.`. They were thirteen and fourteen, were never
charged, and are private individuals; their full names are recoverable from
contemporaneous news coverage in about a minute. ⚠️ **Do not complete them.**
There is an assertion in the verification script that the abbreviations survive.

**2. The author's note is not in the story.** The text arrived with a paragraph
marked *"Lus ceeb toom rau tus kws sau ntawv (tsis yog ib feem ntawm zaj dab
neeg)"* — a note to the writer, explicitly not part of it. It says sentencing law
for juvenile offenders has changed and is still being litigated, so any claim
about where either man is **now** must be re-checked against court and Wisconsin
DOC records first.

The text as shipped makes no such claim: every sentence is about what the courts
decided at the time, in the past tense. **That is what keeps it safe to ship, and
it is a property that one helpful-sounding sentence would destroy.** Recorded
here because the note itself does not travel with the data.

**3. The content warning is in the `blurb`**, because that is the only field the
library card renders before a reader opens anything:

> *Green Bay, 1998 — a true case. Contains the killing of a child; not for young
> readers.*

blurb keeps its short version, because the card still has to warn before a tap.

## Making the killing harder to read

Asked for on 2026-09-13: the death should land as what it was — torture, the
drop, the impact, the fact that he was alive for a little while afterward, the
medical cause — while staying respectful. Six lines were added and two rewritten.

⚠️ **Every one of those Hmong lines is MINE.** Up to this point the rule in this
repo had held without exception: the Hmong is the author's and I do not write
any. This is the first text where I have. They are marked at the site and they
are the lines to read first:

| where | the line |
|---|---|
| ¶11 | `lawv tsim txom nws ntev, ib kauj ruam zuj zus` — they tortured him at length, one step at a time |
| ¶12 | `nkawd yoj nws ntev txaus kom nws paub` — they swung him long enough that he knew |
| ¶13 | `nws lub cev tsoo rau saum thaj av cement… nrog lub zog loj kawg nkaus` |
| ¶13 | `Lub suab tsoo ntawd nrov thoob plaws lub chaw nres tsheb.` |
| ¶14 | `Zong tseem ua pa… tseem muaj sia nyob ib nyuag ntu luv ua ntej nws tuag` |
| ¶15 | `raug mob loj heev—kev raug mob uas cov kws kho mob hu ua craniocerebral trauma` |
| ¶16 | `Lawv tso nws pw nws ib leeg hauv qab.` — they left him lying there alone |

Six new words are glossed with them: `tsim txom`, `tsoo`, `ua pa`, `zog`,
`kawg nkaus`, and `craniocerebral trauma` itself.

**What "respectful" meant in practice**, since it is the hard half of that brief:

- **Nothing invented.** Every added fact — the forty-five feet, the concrete, the
  brief survival, craniocerebral trauma — is in the record. The horror is
  supplied by what happened, not by decoration around it.
- **No description of his body.** The autopsy line says *catastrophically
  injured* and names the medical cause. It does not itemise. The clinical term is
  doing deliberate work: it is the most brutal sentence in the text and the least
  lurid one.
- **The last line of the scene is about abandonment, not injury.** *They left him
  lying there alone* is where the paragraph ends, because that is the fact the
  family had to live with.
- **Two lines are about what he knew**, not what was done to him — *he knew he
  was going to fall.* That is the sentence that makes it torture rather than an
  accident, and it needs no physical detail at all.

> The line I was drawing: **make the reader unable to look away from the choice
> those two made, not from the body of a thirteen-year-old.**

⚠️ **The paragraph count did not change — 45 — and that is not luck.** Every
addition went INSIDE an existing paragraph. All thirteen quiz `because` fields
cite paragraph numbers, so adding a 46th paragraph mid-text would have silently
re-pointed every citation after it. Re-verified after the edit: all 13 still
land.

## The English source arrived, and eleven things were wrong

Then the author supplied **the English account the Hmong was translated from**,
and asked whether it would help. It did. **Eleven discrepancies**, and the two
worst were mine inventing detail the record already had.

### The one that mattered most

### Two invented lines came out

The ones I wrote on 2026-09-13 to make the death land harder. **The record was
more specific than my reconstruction, and more devastating for being specific:**
*on his back, twelve feet from the base of the ramp* does what *with tremendous
force* was reaching for. And my *"the sound carried through the whole ramp"* was
doing a job the source already does better, from a named witness in the next
paragraph — Heraly hearing *"a bag of cement hitting the pavement."*

> **Invented atmosphere competes with sourced fact and loses.** That is the
> lesson, and it is worth more than the two lines.

### The dropped glossary term was not surplus

The first draft cut **Lub Tsev Hais Plaub Rov Hais Dua** (Court of Appeals) from
the author's terminology table, reasoning that the phrase never appears in the
text. It did not appear **because Crapeau's appeal was missing** — the term was
pointing at a part of the story I had left out.

⚠️ **A supplied glossary term that the text does not use is evidence the TEXT is
incomplete, not that the term is.** The author built that table from the whole
account; an unused entry is a gap report.

### What the verifier caught that the checkers did not

Adding Crapeau's appeal created a new paragraph 35, so every `because` after it
shifted by one. Three citations were re-pointed (36→37, 39→40, 43→44) and then
re-verified. Two more things surfaced in the same pass:

- **q-zong-2's quoted evidence went stale** the moment `Zou` was added to the
  line it quotes. The quote no longer appeared in the paragraph it cited.
- **`ua pa` (to breathe) became an orphan gloss** when its only line became the
  faint-pulse line. `check-reading.mjs` did **not** catch it — its rule is that
  *one* word of a glossary phrase must appear, and `ua` is everywhere.

Both are the same species as the judge/police error: correct-looking data that no
longer describes the text. Neither throws.

## Third pass: nothing summarised away

*"add in full, ensure everything is transparent"* — 2026-09-13. The second pass
had still been making editorial choices about what a language app should show.
They are all reversed.

### Ninham's threats, verbatim

The first draft said he "threatened the juveniles who had spoken to the police."
That sentence was doing a lot of hiding. The record is four specific threats,
and they are now four lines:

- the **life** of Judge Richard J. Dietz, presiding over his case;
- a **"drive-by" at Jeffrey P.'s house**;
- to **"rape and kill" Amanda G.** — fourteen years old;
- to **arrange the killing of Richard Crapeau's sister**.

⚠️ **The content warning had to widen with the text.** The blurb said *"contains

### Where they are now — the one claim under a standing warning

Crapeau at **Racine Correctional Institution**, ~28 years served; Ninham at **Fox
Lake Correctional Institution**, one of the few people in Wisconsin history
sentenced to life without parole for a crime committed at fourteen.

⚠️ **This is precisely what the author's own writer's note said to verify first.**
I raised it, the author said add it in full, so it is in — with the as-of anchor
written **inside the Hmong**:

> *Raws li cov ntaub ntawv txog xyoo 2026…* — "According to records as of 2026…"

That is the transparent form of a time-bound fact: the reader sees it is dated
rather than timeless. **Custody placements change and juvenile sentencing is
still being litigated**, so this needs re-checking against Wisconsin DOC before
each release, not once. It is in `notes/TODO.md` as a recurring item rather
than a one-off box.

### A third paragraph insert, a third citation shift

The "where they are now" paragraph is a new ¶39, so everything from the old 39
moved up one. Two citations re-pointed (40→41, 44→45) and re-verified. That is
**three separate paragraph inserts on this text, each one silently re-pointing
citations**, and the only reason none of them shipped broken is that the check

⚠️ **`check-reading.mjs` cannot catch this** and should not be extended to — it
validates that an answer is among its options, which is a property of one
question. "Does this quote appear in the paragraph it cites" is a property of the
question AND the prose together, and it is the one that breaks every time the
prose moves.

## Fourth pass: faithfulness, line by line

*"relook through the english text, and try to make the hmong text as faithful as
possible."* Twenty-eight more edits. The pattern in almost all of them is the
same one, and it is worth naming:

> **I had been summarising quotes into reported speech, and the quote was always
> the stronger thing.**

### Six quotes that had been paraphrases

Crapeau's statement to police was the worst case. It had shipped as *"Richard
Crapeau told the police that he was angry with his mother, and so he wanted to
fight."* The record is his own words, and they are the most damning lines in the
paragraph:

> *"As we walked up Webster (Avenue) I saw this Asian kid riding a bike toward
> us. I was still upset with my mom and I wanted to fight or see a fight so I
> told Omer, 'Let's mess with this kid.' I didn't know who this kid was and
> didn't recognize him."*

Also now verbatim: Zong's *"Why are you trying to hurt me?"*; Crapeau's *"we'd be
busted"* and *"we pretty much knew nobody would say anything"*; Seng Say's full
statement of the Hmong belief, including *"we ask the Court, who is the only one
to have the power to set free the spirit of our beloved son"*; counsel's *"In
terms of the abuse of a child, I'm not going to argue that"*; and Ninham being
told to *"drop him."*

### The beating did not stop, and the text said it did

Asked for directly. The draft compressed the whole of the fifth floor into one
abstract line about torture. The record is specific, and **the specifics are the
torture**: they kept beating him *while he was pinned*, and **beat him harder
when he squirmed**, while he cried, screamed and begged. Four lines now, where
there was one.

Same correction in the swing: Richard let go of the ankles **after they had
swung him out over the wall**, not as a separate moment. The release was the end
of the swing — one action, and the text had it as two.

### Two of my own lines came out for being mine

- *"They left him lying there alone."* The record says something better because
  it is sourced: **they never checked on his condition.**
- *"He was alive a short while longer before he died."* The faint pulse and
  *"physicians were unable to revive him"* say it without me saying it.

them written to add weight, every one of them displaced by a fact that carried
more. I do not think this is a coincidence about this text.~~

⚠️ **That conclusion was wrong, and the author said so within the hour.** See
*Fifth pass* below. The lines that were genuinely redundant did deserve to go;

### Order matters, twice

- **He screamed AS he fell.** The draft had him fall, then scream — two
  sentences, wrong sequence, and the second one killed the first.
- **The landing moved paragraphs.** *On his back, on the paved exit lane, twelve
  feet from the base* belongs with the rescue crew, where the record puts it. The
  fall paragraph now ends on Crapeau's *"just sailed out over the wall"*, which
  is where it should end.

The grocery store (he was going to one); *bumped* rather than *struck*; chased

## Fifth pass: the over-correction, reversed

> *"you should've kept some of the dramatization to iterate the horrificness of
> the crime, instead of making it super super verbatim — I only asked to include
> the missing parts."*

Correct, and worth writing down properly because it is a failure mode, not a
slip.

**The brief was "add the missing facts." I added them AND deleted the authored
lines in the same pass**, on a faithfulness argument nobody had made. Nothing in
the request implied the two were in competition. I invented the tradeoff and then
resolved it against myself.

> **Verbatim and vivid were never the two options.** The record supplies what
> happened. The authored lines supply the weight of it. A text about a
> thirteen-year-old thrown off a building needs both, and stripping one to
> "respect" the other produces a police report.

### What came back, and where it sits

Every restored line is **layered onto the record, never in place of it** — the
sourced sentence comes first, the authored one follows and is marked `AUTHORED`
in the data so the distinction survives the next edit.

| ¶ | the record says | and then |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | he squirmed, Richard punched him harder, he begged | *"They tortured a thirteen-year-old boy at length, one step at a time — and **every step of it was a choice**."* |
| 13 | he screamed as he fell onto the pavement | *"Forty-five feet below, his body struck the concrete with tremendous force."* |
| 14 | rescue personnel detected a **faint pulse** | *"Which means Zong was still alive — alive a short while longer — after the fall."* |
| 15 | died from **craniocerebral trauma** | *"…means the skull and the brain destroyed together — a thirteen-year-old's head, broken on concrete."* |
| 16 | they never checked on him, they ran | *"They left Zong lying alone in the dark, at the base of the parking ramp."* |

⚠️ **¶14 and ¶15 are the ones that justify the whole idea.** *"A faint pulse"*
and *"craniocerebral trauma"* are the two most important facts in the story and
both are **invisible to a reader who does not already know what they mean**. The
authored line does not decorate them — it translates them. A faint pulse means
he did not die on impact. Craniocerebral trauma means a child's skull broken on
concrete.

**In a language-learning app this is not optional.** A reader working through
Hmong at intermediate level cannot be expected to decode a clinical Latinate term
in their second language and feel what it says.

### The rule, stated so it survives

> Sourced fact first, authored weight second, each marked. **Delete an authored
> line only when a sourced line says the same thing** — not because it is
> authored.

Two of the four deletions met that test and stay deleted: *"the sound carried
through the whole ramp"* (Heraly's *"bag of cement"* is the same beat, from a
witness) and the original vaguer *"struck the concrete with tremendous force"*,
which is back only now that the record's geometry sits beside it in ¶14 doing the
other half of the job.

## Sixth pass: the gate, the header, the moral, and 26 questions

### The header was unreadable, and the numbers say why

The masthead's background is the **genre cover**, so the same cream text sits on
a different colour per shelf — and every colour flips per theme. Measured (WCAG
2.1, text composited over the cover, worst of the three themes):

|           | α.60 | α.70 | α.75 | α.85 | α.90 | α1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clay-600  | **2.7** | 3.1 | 3.4 | 4.0 | 4.3 | 4.9 |
| ocean-700 | 3.7 | 4.9 | 5.6 | 7.3 | 7.9 | 9.3 |
| stone-700 | 3.5 | 4.5 | 5.1 | 6.5 | 7.3 | 9.0 |
| stone-800 | 4.0 | 5.3 | 6.1 | 8.3 | 9.6 | 12.3 |

4.5 is the AA floor for small text. ⚠️ **Only full opacity clears it on every
shelf** — and `clay-600`, which carries the two oldest stories in the library,
put the genre eyebrow at **2.7**. This was never a Zong Vang bug; it has been
there since the reader was built, on the shelf with the most traffic.

Every opacity modifier is gone from that header. **The hierarchy never needed
them**: 11px tracked uppercase, 4xl serif, 15px medium, 13px medium — four
distinct sizes and weights already. Opacity was doing the same job a second time
and charging legibility for it. The level chip went `bg-cream-50/15` → `/28`,
which on clay-600 is the difference between a chip and a rumour.

> The transferable bit: **when the background is data, contrast is a property of
> every row, not of the design.** One cover being fine says nothing about four.

### A content gate, at last

`src/components/common/StoryWarningModal.jsx` + `src/lib/storyWarning.js`.
Driven by an optional `warning` field on any story; omit it and no gate appears.

- **Not an RN `<Modal>`**, for the two reasons `LimitModal.jsx` already
  documents: its buttons render under the system nav bar on this build, and
  themed NativeWind classes resolve to **transparent** inside one. Every colour
  is resolved by hand — a class that fails to resolve renders invisible, which
  on a content warning is the worst available failure.
- **No scrim-tap-to-dismiss**, unlike every other overlay here. Tapping outside
  is how you dismiss something opened by accident; it is not how anyone should
  get past a mature-content gate. Two buttons, both labelled.
- **Three states, not two.** `warningAck === null` means storage has not
  answered yet and is *not* "not accepted" — rendering the gate during that gap
  would flash the warning at a returning reader every single time.
- **Acknowledgement is its own storage key**, never a field on
  `readingProgress`. The bookmark is rewritten on every scroll; a consent
  record must not be at the mercy of a write that frequent.

The scorpion and the frog, then five lines drawing it out — *they want nothing

look like monsters; they look like a group of kids walking down a street after

### 13 → 26 questions

The rule held and got harder: **no question asks about the violence as
spectacle.** At thirteen that was easy; at twenty-six the easy factual material
runs out and the pull is toward quizzing injuries. The three that touch the
assault each ask about a **choice** — what they called it ("Chicken"), what
Richard said, what Omer was holding when he let go.

Two came from the author:

- **"Why did the group attack and kill him?"** — with *"Zong was in a Hmong
  gang… and threw gang signs to provoke them"* as a distractor. That is the
  real-world victim-blaming narrative, stated so the text can refute it.
- **"What did Zong's parents testify?"** — with *"they had been allies of the
  Pathet Lao"* as an option. ⚠️ **That is a distractor, and the reading was
  flagged rather than assumed.** The Pathet Lao were the communist side; Hmong
  families fled Laos largely *because* they had fought against them. Marking it
  correct would state the opposite of the community's own history inside a Hmong
  app. It is a good distractor for exactly that reason.

### The sentencing record, from the opinion itself

The author supplied `State v. Ninham` (2008AP1139) ¶¶24-30 verbatim, which
**corrected a fact the story had wrong**:

| shipped | the opinion |
|---|---|
| the four extra counts were **dismissed as part of the jury's verdict** | the **State moved** to dismiss them at the sentencing hearing on **29 June 2000**, and asked that all four be **read in** |

⚠️ "Dismissed" and "read in" are close to opposites in effect — read in means the
court may weigh them in sentencing with no conviction. The draft had the
mechanism *and* the timing wrong, and it read perfectly.

Four new paragraphs: the hearing, the PSI (Ninham at 16 still denying it; an
extremely dysfunctional family; a "serious substance abuser"; a member of the
Menominee Indian Tribe), the court's three factors, and the sentence — including
the **five years consecutive** on the child-abuse count that was missing
entirely.

The court's own words carry it: the offense "beyond description" and "horrific";
Ninham "a frightening young man", "a child of the street who knew what he was
doing"; and the line that answers the first paragraph of the story —

> *"Society needs to know, and especially this community needs to know, that you
> can send your child to the grocery store and expect to see him again."*

### Five citations shifted, and one checker nearly died

Four new paragraphs moved everything after ¶31 by one each; **five `because`
fields re-pointed**, all caught by comparing quote against paragraph.

The age check fired on `raug kaw **tsib xyoos**` — five years' *imprisonment*,

⚠️ **And the negative test for it was itself broken.** A shell heredoc ate one

## Seventh pass: every word, on tap

> *"I tapped `nees` — it led to horse, which is not correct. It's supposed to
> be `nees nkaum`, twenty."*

Right, and it was one of about fifty. **Coverage was never the measure.** 85%
said how many taps got *an* answer; it said nothing about whether the answer was
the right one, and `nees` resolved perfectly to "horse" on every line it
appeared on.

So I ran the real `lookupWord` over every distinct token in the story and
printed what came back, with the surrounding text. Three families of error fell
out, and none of them is a typo.

### 1. Compounds, where the dictionary answers the other half

```
nees     "horse"                        · here: half of nees nkaum, twenty
plaub    "four"                         · here: also rooj plaub, a legal case
kaw      "to close"                     · here: raug kaw, imprisoned
hluas    "niam hluas, younger sister"   · here: tub hluas, a young man

laj      "ntsa laj kab, fence"          · here: laj thawj, a reason
kho      "Hloov Kho, amendment"         · here: kws kho mob, a doctor
```

### 2. ⚠️ The author's terminology table backfired through tier 3

This is the one worth reading twice, because **the table is the best thing in the
file and none of it should be removed.**

Tier 3 answers a tapped word with *a glossary phrase containing it*, first match
wins. The table's entries are the longest phrases in the glossary — `kev tua
neeg txhob txwm thawj theem`, `kaw mus tas sim neej yam tsis muaj cai thov tawm
ntxov` — so between them they contain a large share of the commonest words in
Hmong. Measured:

| tapped | ×  | answered with |
|---|---|---|
| `neeg` | 43 | "first-degree intentional homicide" |
| `raug` | 36 | "parole eligible after fifty years" |
| `yam` | 23 | "life without parole" |
| `chaw` | 21 | "the Green Bay Police Department" |
| `menyuam` | 19 | "the Brown County Juvenile Detention Center" |
| `kho` | 6 | "the Eighth Amendment" |

> **This is "every `tus` meant `tus xov`" returning by a different door.**
> There, the phrase tier outranked the dictionary. Here the dictionary has no
> entry at all, so the phrase tier is the only thing answering — and a longer
> glossary makes it *worse*, which is the opposite of the intuition.

### 3. Words with no answer at all

Ordinary Hmong missing from a 600-word dictionary: `ntxiv`, `kam`, `khw`,
`dim`, `txwv`, `pub`, `tsuam`, `xaiv`, `nqes`, `npaj`, `tuav`,
`hem`, `lees`, `koom`, `ntseeg`…

### The fix, and why it is tier 1

**Every correction is a story-glossary entry for the exact tapped word.** Tier 1
beats the dictionary and beats the phrase fallback, and it is scoped to this
text — `nees` still means *horse* everywhere else in the app, which it should.
Editing `vocabulary.js` would have been right for a few of these and
catastrophic for the rest.

⚠️ **Each entry names the compound.** "twenty" alone teaches nothing:

```js
{ hmong: 'nees', english: 'in “nees nkaum” — twenty. NOT “horse”, which is what
  this word means on its own.' }
```

The reader needs to learn that the word under their finger is **half of
something**. That is the same principle as tier 4 returning `tag kis` rather
than `kis`.

⚠️ **Two entries carry BOTH senses, because the story uses both.** `caum` is
*to chase* in the parking ramp and *-ty* in "plaub caum tsib feet". `nkaum` is
*to hide* in the fable and *twenty* in the date. An entry that picks one is wrong
half the times it fires.

### Where it landed

**87 → 193 glossary entries. Answered taps 85% → 90%**, and the remaining 10% is
now almost entirely proper nouns:

```
zong(68) omer(29) richard(24) ninham(19) crapeau(10) jeffrey(7) …
```

⚠️ **Those stay unanswered on purpose.** Names came out of the dictionary on
2026-09-13; putting them in a glossary would feed them straight back into the
quiz and the review queue.

### What this says about the metric

The coverage number in the fifth pass was 85% and it was not wrong — it was
**measuring the wrong thing**, and I reported it as though it meant the reader
was well served. It meant a tap produced text.

> **For a lookup, "did it answer" and "was it right" are different numbers, and
> only one of them can be computed without reading the output.** The audit
> script is in the scratchpad pattern used here; the durable version of it is
> exercise 3 in `learning/concepts/lookup-ranking-and-fallbacks.md`, which
> asked for exactly this and had not been done.

## Eighth pass: the header was invisible for a different reason

The seventh pass raised the header text to full opacity and reported the
contrast numbers. The author came back: **still not showing colour.**

At full opacity, cream-50 on stone-700 is **9:1**. That cannot be invisible. So
the foreground was never the problem — **the background was not being painted at
all**, and cream text on the cream paper underneath is exactly what that looks
like.

> ⚠️ **When the fix for an invisible element changes nothing, stop adjusting the
> foreground and ask whether the background is being drawn.** I measured the
> contrast of two colours, one of which was not on screen.

### Why it was not painted

`GENRES[].cover` is a Tailwind class stored in **data**, applied by
interpolating it into a className:

```jsx
<View className={`px-6 pt-7 pb-6 ${genre?.cover}`}>
```

That works only while Tailwind has compiled that exact class — and
`bg-stone-700`'s **only appearance in the entire codebase** is one string in
`src/data/stories.js`. The three older covers were compiled; the new one was
not, and NativeWind drops a class it cannot resolve **silently**, the same way
themed classes vanish inside a `<Modal>`.

⚠️ The comment at the top of `GENRES` already warned about the runtime-built
half of this ("never built by interpolation"). It did not cover the case where
the whole class is a literal *in data* and the consuming screen interpolates it.

### The fix

`src/lib/genreCover.js` — strip `bg-`, read `--c-<rest>` from the theme,
return an `rgb()` string. **An inline style is a value, not a name that has to
have been compiled somewhere else.** A new shelf needs no change: `bg-ocean-700`
finds `--c-ocean-700`.

Applied at **all five places a cover is painted** — the reader masthead, the
reader's progress band, the genre shelf sign, the library's shelf dot, and the
cover tile — because every one of them had the same exposure and four of them
were about to show a blank rectangle on the True accounts shelf.

Two things surfaced while doing it:

- **Two `style` props on one element.** Three of the five already had a
  `style`, so adding another silently discarded the first — JSX keeps the last.
  eslint catches it (`react/jsx-no-duplicate-props`), but on an element where
  the two props set *different* keys the symptom is "my change did nothing".
- **The shelf dot lives in `Shelf()`, not `ReadingsHub()`.** The hook went
  into the wrong component and `libraryTheme` was out of scope at the use site.
  `check-undefined-refs.mjs` caught it.

### And the heading names him

`Qhov xwm txheej` → **`Qhov xwm txheej ntawm Zong Vang`**, at the author's
request. ⚠️ The Hmong is mine; `ntawm` as "of/concerning" reads, but a fluent
reader should confirm it over e.g. `Zong Vang qhov xwm txheej`.

## Ninth pass: the dictionary learns second senses, and two more silent bugs

### Context senses now live in the DICTIONARY, not only in one story

The eighth pass fixed `nees` → "horse" with a story-glossary override, which is
scoped to this text. The author's point: **`thov` means *please* AND *to
plead*, the same way `rau` means six, to/for, and to put on footwear.** That is
a property of the word, not of this story, and it belongs where every reading can
see it.

`wordLookup` already supported it and nothing was using it: when a hmongRPA key
already exists the builder **pushes onto a `senses` array** instead of
overwriting. So a second sense is a second ENTRY, not an edit:

```
thov   please · to plead, to beg, to ask for
kaw    to close · to imprison, to confine — "raug kaw"
siab   tall · liver · the seat of feeling and intent
plaub  four · a legal case — "rooj plaub", "tsev hais plaub"
txim   fault, punishment · to judge, to decide — in "txiav txim"
nees   horse · twenty — only in "nees nkaum"
caum   to chase · "-ty" in a number
```

⚠️ **The hmongRPA must match the existing entry exactly**, or this silently
creates a separate headword instead of a second sense — and the symptom is a
word that keeps giving the old answer. Verified by running the real lookup: 8 of
these now show a "·".

The first entry in file order still owns identity — the id the notebook saves,
the category, the example — so this changes what is displayed and nothing else.

### The dictionary had no countries at all

A strange gap for a diaspora language, and the three that matter most are in this
story's own paragraph about why the Vang family left: **Nplog teb** (Laos),
**Thaib teb** (Thailand), **Tebchaws Meskas** (the United States). Added with
`Suav teb`, `Nyab Laj`, `Fab Kis`, plus `teb chaws`, `xeev` and
`nroog`.

⚠️ Only names I can attest. A guessed transliteration of a country name is worse
than its absence, so the list stops where my confidence does.

### ⚠️ Em dashes were fusing words into untappable tokens

The reader made one tap target per **whitespace**-separated token. The author
writes em dashes closed up, the way the source does:

```
Zong Vang—ib tug tub hluas…        →  token: "Vang—ib"
theem saum toj kawg—theem thib tsib →  token: "kawg—theem"
```

**21 pairs of ordinary words were fused**, and every one of them was untappable
while looking completely normal. `normalizeWord` strips punctuation from the
ENDS of a token and never the middle, so the lookup was handed `kawg—theem`,
found nothing, and said so.

> That is the worst shape a bug can have here: the failure message — *"no entry
> for this word yet"* — is **always plausible** in a dictionary that is openly
> incomplete. It could have sat there for the life of the app.

The split is now `/(s+|[—–])/` and the separator guard accepts a dash, so the
dash still prints where it was and simply is not pressable. This fixes every
story, not just this one.

### An "are you sure" on the way out

`src/components/common/LeaveReadingModal.jsx`, on the Android hardware back
button and back gesture.

- ⚠️ **The scrim DOES dismiss this one**, unlike the content warning two sections
  up. They look alike and are opposites: a content gate must not be escapable by
  a stray tap, but an "are you sure" that traps you is a worse bug than the thing
  it prevents. Tapping outside means *stay* — the safe answer.
- ⚠️ **A ref shadows the state.** `BackHandler`'s listener closes over the
  values it was registered with, so a listener registered once would read the
  first render's `leaving` forever and re-open the modal on the press that was
  answering it.
- **It says progress is kept, because it is** — `readingProgress.js` bookmarks
  the offset and every revealed line. A confirmation that implies loss where
  there is none trains people to dismiss confirmations.

⚠️ **Scope: back only.** The breadcrumbs and the tab bar are ordinary `<Link>`s
and are not intercepted. Back is the gesture people actually leave a reader with;
the others would each need their own guard.

## The quiz had a design problem before it had a content problem

A comprehension quiz over a real child's death can turn the worst minutes of a
family's life into trivia — *how far did he fall, which one let go first*. The
form invites it, because those are the easiest facts to write four options for.

**No question asks about the violence for its own sake.** They ask what the text
establishes: who he was, that he was a stranger to them, what Richard said his
own reason was, what the courts decided, what the family's belief required, and
how he is remembered. The two that touch the assault ask *where he ran* and *what
the group did afterward* — both about the record, not the injuries.

> The rule this text taught: **in non-fiction about a real person, the quiz is
> not neutral.** Whatever it asks about is what it says the story was about.

## Section headings are now visibly headings

The author's five section breaks — *Qhov xwm txheej*, *Tom qab qhov xwm txheej*,
*Omer Ninham rooj plaub*, *Kev thov rov hais plaub dua*, *Nco txog Zong Vang* —
were shipping as body prose, indistinguishable from the sentence above them.

They carry `heading: true` now, and the reader scales the type from it:

```js
const fontSize = line.heading ? Math.round(size.body * 1.45) : size.body
```

Two decisions inside that one line:

1. **A heading is still an ordinary LINE.** No separate heading component, so it
   keeps everything a line has — tap to reveal its English, long-press any word
   for the dictionary, the same paragraph number in the margin. A heading that
   quietly lost tap-to-reveal would read as a bug, not as a style.
2. **Scaled from `size`, never a fixed px.** `size` is the reader's text-size
   preference. A hard `26` would ignore the stepper on the one line that most
   wants to be bigger.

⚠️ The looked-up-word chip is its own `<Text>` inside an inline `<View>`, so it
carries its own size — left on `size.body` it would have SHRUNK the one word in
a heading someone had just long-pressed. It reads `fontSize` now too.

The story title went **text-3xl → text-4xl**, also at the author's request. The
heading ladder puts text-3xl on a sub-screen and text-4xl on a hub masthead, and
this screen is a sub-screen by route and a masthead by job: a title page you look
at once before committing to twenty minutes. Recorded at the site as a deliberate
exception, so nobody "fixes" the ladder later.

## Why the drafted glosses are in the story, not the dictionary

Same reasoning, and it is the `rau` lesson from earlier today. A story glossary
is **scoped to one text**. A gloss that is right in context — `pob zeb` as
"concrete" here, not "rock" — cannot leak a wrong sense into the dictionary, the
quiz, or the SRS the way a dictionary entry can. See
`learning/concepts/lookup-ranking-and-fallbacks.md`.

The cost is that the work does not benefit other stories. For sixty context-bound
glosses on a courtroom text, that is the right trade.
